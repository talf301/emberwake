Interface guide

Emberwake

The phone interface of Emberwake, a solo deck-building hex adventure, screen by screen: what each control does, which engine call backs it, and where the code lives. Written for the owner and for the next agent to work on the UI.

Code public/app.mjs, public/style.css Rules public/engine/engine.js, data.js Shots 390×760 pt, headless Chrome, tools/shot.sh
Section 1

Overview

The game is played in portrait, one-handed. The game screen is a single column built by renderGame(). Everything else (card details, sites, the hero, the menu, choices, the result) is a bottom sheet built by renderSheet() and laid over the game. Tapping the dark backdrop closes a sheet. A sheet that needs a decision (a pending choice or the game-over result) is modal: it has no grab bar and the backdrop does not close it.

How a tap becomes a state change

Every tap goes through one delegated click listener on document. It finds the closest element with a data-act attribute and calls the function of that name in the ACTS table, passing the element's dataset. Disabled buttons are ignored. Map hexes are the exception: they use pointer events (onHexTap), so the same gesture can also pan and pinch the map.

data-act="play"→ACTS.play(dataset)→dispatch(action)→E.act(G, action)→save()→render()

dispatch() calls the engine. If the engine refuses, its error text is shown as a toast and nothing else happens. If it accepts, the new log line (if any) is toasted, an undo snapshot is kept, the open sheet closes (unless the call passed keepSheet), the state is saved to localStorage under emberwake.save.v1, and the whole screen re-renders. The interface computes no rules: it asks the engine what is legal and draws that.

Section 3

The game screen

Built by renderGame() from topHTML, the stage, stripHTML, hintText, actionsHTML, partyHTML and handHTML.

Top bar (topHTML)

Six round pips, the day or night and turn counter, three stat pills (renown, armor, standing) that all open the Hero sheet, and the menu button. Renown reads renown /next L level; the threshold disappears at the top level. Standing shows a sign when positive (+4 in the mid-game shot below).

Stage: the map (mapSVG)

  • Drag to pan, pinch to zoom (0.55× to 2.2×). A drag longer than 8 px is not a tap. The view recentres when the hero moves; the compass button recentres on demand.
  • Terrain art per hex: plains, forest, hills, marsh, ash wastes, mountain, lake. Unexplored hexes are drawn dark with no art.
  • Site badges. Gold ring: neutral or friendly. Red ring: defended. Green ring: conquered. Dimmed: used up (a visited wayshrine, a cleared camp, lair, barrow or ruin). A small red number at the top right counts the defenders when there are several. A camp or lair with defenders shows the first foe's glyph instead of the site icon.
  • When the hero stands on a site, the badge moves up and the hero token sits below it.

Resource strip (stripHTML)

Move, Sway, and Heal (only while Heal is banked) are hidden during a battle. Essence shows stored essence as coloured dots, then sun: (or dusk: at night) and the two aspects the sky offers this turn, until that free essence is used. Shards shows stored shards as diamonds. Deck counts cards left. Essence and Shards open the essence sheet; Deck opens the cards sheet. A chip at zero is dimmed. The strip scrolls sideways when it overflows.

Hint line (hintText)

One sentence chosen by state, in priority order: the battle phase; the last night; an empty deck (“End the round to reshuffle”); standing on a trading site; already acted; nothing played yet; otherwise “Tap a glowing hex to move, or a red hex to attack.”

Party row (partyHTML) and hand (handHTML)

Companions are solid pills; a spent companion is dim, a wounded one turns red with a ✖. Skills are dashed pills, dim once used for the turn or round. Each opens its own sheet.

The hand overlaps cards so all of them fit: the step between cards is 50 to 88 px, from the window width up to 520 px. A dimmed card has no playable effect now (no Play, Empower, Improvise or Unleash is legal), but it can still be channelled or discarded. Tap any card to open its sheet.

Section 4

Cards

Card face: cardHTML(). Card sheet: cardSheet(i), options from E.cardOptions(G, i).

Card anatomy

  • Aspect colour: Ember red, Frost blue, Verdant green, Radiant gold, Relic purple, Wound dark red.
  • Name at the top, glyph in the middle.
  • Light box: the basic effect. Dark box: the empowered effect, led by one coloured pip per essence it costs. A relic's dark box is led by “Unleash:”.
  • Corner mark: ▲ advanced, ✦ arcana, ◆ relic. Starter cards have none.

Card sheet buttons

  • Play: the basic effect. Arcana cost 1 essence to play.
  • Empower: the strong effect, paid with essence of the card's aspect (1 for most cards, 2 to overcast an arcana). A relic's second effect is Unleash instead, which destroys the relic.
  • Channel: discard for one essence of the card's aspect. Not offered for relics.
  • Improvise: four small buttons, each a discard for 1 of Move, Sway, Attack or Block.
  • Discard.

Each button is one entry from E.cardOptions. A disabled button prints the engine's reason (o.why) under its label. An enabled button that would waste part of its effect prints “Part of this has no use right now and would be lost” (o.partial). The gold (primary) style goes to Play when it is legal, otherwise to Empower.

Section 5

Moving

Glow and badges from E.moveOptions(G); tap handled by onHexTap, which dispatches {type:'move', to}.
  • Tap a glowing hex to move. The map recentres on the hero. Tapping a grey dashed hex toasts “Needs N Move (you have M)”.
  • Tap any other revealed hex to open the hex sheet (hexSheet): terrain, Move cost by day and by night, the site's rules, known defenders, and whether it can be attacked. An unexplored hex says “Move closer to reveal this land.”
  • Night tints the map. Sight drops from 2 hexes to 1. Forest costs 4 and ash wastes 3.
  • Leaving a hex next to a reaver camp or wyrm lair costs +1 (see the 3-cost badges in section 6).
Section 6

Starting a fight

Red hexes from E.attackOptions(G). The Attack button dispatches {type:'attack', hex}.

Camps, lairs, keeps, spires and the citadel are attacked from an adjacent hex. Barrows and ruins are attacked from inside the hex. Winning a walled site moves the hero in. Starting a battle spends the turn's action and sets Move to 0. It also bumps state.irrev, so the undo history is cleared.

Section 7

Battle

Built by battleHTML(), which replaces the map in the stage. Previews from E.strikePower, E.blockPower, E.blockNeeded, E.canTarget.

Phase tabs across the top show progress. Foe cards show glyph, name, armor, attack (red for Ember, blue for Frost), trait and resistance tags, and a stamp when Defeated, Guarded or Snared. Under the foes is the readout: a live preview of what would happen, the sums behind it, and the phase's commit button. The action bar's main button moves to the next phase.

Volley. Play Ranged or Siege cards, tap foes to target them, then Strike. Behind walls (keeps, spires, the citadel) only Siege counts, and the readout says so. Warded foes cannot be targeted yet; tapping one toasts “Warded: cannot be targeted in the Volley”.

Strike rules the readout applies
  • All attack counts in the Strike: Ranged, Siege and Melee. Attack played in an earlier phase is kept for the Strike.
  • A strike spends all attack of the kinds that count, even if more than needed.
  • If any chosen target resists an element, all attack of that element is halved for the whole strike (rounded down at the end). The readout adds “Ember attack is halved by resistance.”
  • Defeated foes stay defeated. Survivors keep the site; you may return.
Section 8

Choices and rewards

Built by pendingSheet() from the top of G.pending, options from E.pendingOptions. Each button dispatches {type:'choose', index}.

A pending choice always wins over any other sheet and blocks map taps, End turn and new plays until resolved. The kinds:

  • Choose one: a card with alternative effects (for example Steady Hand: Attack 2 or Block 2).
  • Which aspect: gain an essence or a shard of an aspect you pick.
  • Ready which companion?
  • Snare which foe? or Which foe gets Armor −N? during battle.
  • Rewards: a skill on even levels (Learn), an advanced card on level-up, an arcana from a spire's library, a relic from a barrow, ruin or lair. Cards taken go on top of the deck.

Several pending entries stack. Levelling up queues a skill and an advanced card; the advanced card shows first.

Section 9

Sites and trading

The Site button opens hexSheet for the hero's hex, which appends shopHTML() (from E.shopOptions). Companion sheet: unitSheet(i), options from E.unitOptions.

Villages, sanctums, conquered keeps and conquered spires trade. Buying anything sets Move to 0 and counts as the turn's action, but the sheet stays open (keepSheet) so you can keep buying. You cannot trade on a turn you fought (“You fought this turn. Come back next turn to trade.”).

Section 10

Wounds and recovery

Wound sheet: the wound branch of cardSheet. Recover: recoverSheet(), dispatching {type:'recover', index}.
  • Wounds are dead cards in hand. Holding as many as your hand limit knocks you out and you lose the rest of your hand.
  • Tap a wound to read its sheet. When Heal is banked (from Field Dressing, a healer or a companion), it offers Heal this wound (1 Heal), which removes it for good.
  • Recover uses the whole turn. If the hand is all wounds, the sheet becomes “Catch your breath” and sets aside up to 2.
  • With a wound in hand and nothing played, End turn is blocked until you play a card, discard one, or recover.
Section 11

Hero, cards, menu, rules

The Chronicle (logSheet) lists the game log, newest first. It opens from the book button on the map, the menu, or the result sheet.

Section 13

Worked examples

Tap by tap. Numbers are from data.js and were checked by running the engine under Node with the same setup as the dev scenarios.

1. Move two hexes with an empowered card

Seed 7, Kaela, first turn. Hand: Fair Words, Field Dressing, Trailcraft, Hewing Blow, Trailcraft. Shots 02, 03, 04.

  1. Tap the first Trailcraft (third card). Its sheet opens (shot 03).
  2. Tap Empower. It costs one Verdant essence. You have none stored, so the free daytime sky essence (Radiant or Verdant) pays. Move 4
  3. The sheet closes. Four plains hexes glow with green 2 badges; Essence drops to 0; Undo lights (shot 04).
  4. Tap a glowing hex. The hero moves, the map recentres, and new land is revealed. Move 4 − 2 = 2 Because a reveal changed state.irrev, Undo is cleared.
  5. Tap another glowing hex with a green 2. Move 0 Hexes you can no longer afford (one costs 3 after the first step east) show grey dashed badges.
  6. Tap End turn. You keep the unplayed cards and draw back to your hand limit.

2. A whole battle against Wolf Riders and Cinder Hounds

The dev=combat scenario: Kaela (armor 2, hand limit 5), one Shieldmaidens companion (armor 5). Hand: Hunter's Shot, Shieldwork, Hewing Blow, Cinder Lash, Sidestep, Steady Hand. Shots 06–10.

FoeArmorAttackNotes
Wolf Riders43Swift (block needs double). Renown 3.
Cinder Hounds34 EmberResists Ember. Renown 3.
  1. Attack. From the camp's hex sheet, tap Attack (shot 06). The stage becomes the battle panel at Volley.
  2. Volley. Tap Hunter's Shot → Play: Ranged 2. Tap Cinder Hounds: Power 2 vs Armor 3 in red (shot 07). Wolf Riders need 4, both together 7. Ranged 2 cannot kill anything, so do not strike: Ranged that is not spent carries into the Strike. Tap To Guard ▸.
  3. Guard, Wolf Riders. They are selected first. Swift doubles the block needed: 3 × 2 = 6. Shieldwork gives Block 3 of 6 (shot 08). Even empowered (Block 5) it falls short.
  4. Guard, Cinder Hounds. Their attack is Ember, so only Frost and Pure block count in full; plain block is halved. Shieldwork's 3 becomes ⌊3 ÷ 2⌋ = 1 of 4. Sidestep empowered gives Frost Block 4, but it needs Frost essence, which neither the day sky nor Kaela's Ember shard provides.
  5. Neither foe can be guarded well, so tap Take the hits ▸. (In the shots for the later phases the scenario skipped the earlier plays, so Hunter's Shot and Shieldwork are back in hand and Ranged reads 0.)
  6. Damage, Wolf Riders: 3. With armor 2, each 2 damage or part of it is a wound: ⌈3 ÷ 2⌉ = 2 wounds. Or tap Shieldmaidens absorb 5: their armor 5 covers all 3, and they become wounded (shot 09). Choose the Shieldmaidens.
  7. Damage, Cinder Hounds: 4. The Shieldmaidens are used, so tap Take 2 wounds: ⌈4 ÷ 2⌉ = 2 wounds. Two Wound cards enter the hand and the battle moves to Strike.
  8. Strike. Play Hewing Blow (Attack 2) and Cinder Lash (Ember Attack 3). Tap Wolf Riders: 2 + 3 = Power 5 vs Armor 4 in green (shot 10). Tap Strike. Wolf Riders are defeated, renown +3.
  9. Why not both? Adding the Hounds raises the need to 4 + 3 = 7, and because the Hounds resist Ember, the Ember part is halved for the whole strike: 2 + 3 ÷ 2 = 3.5 → 3 vs 7. The strike would also spend all the attack. Strike mixed foes one at a time.
  10. Level up mid-battle. Renown reaches 3, the level 2 threshold. Two modal reward sheets open: an advanced card, then a skill (section 8). Resolve them to continue.
  11. Finish the Hounds. The day's sky essence is still unused. Tap Steady Hand → Empower (Radiant): Attack 3 vs Armor 3, physical, not resisted. Strike. The camp is cleared, Standing +1, and the action bar reads End battle. If you had kept the Ranged 2 from the Volley, it would also count here.

3. Recruit and heal at a sanctum

The dev=shop scenario: Kaela on a sanctum, Peasant Levy (spent) in a command of 2, a wound in hand, Rallying Banner already empowered for Sway 6. Shot 12.

  1. Tap Site. The Sanctum sheet lists 6 Sway, the Healer for 2, Hedge Healer for 5, and two 7-Sway companions marked “Not enough Sway”.
  2. 6 Sway covers the healer or the Hedge Healer, not both (2 + 5 = 7). Close the sheet and play Fair Words first: Sway 6 + 2 = 8. Sway cards must be played before trading.
  3. Tap Site again, then the Healer's 2 Sway. The wound leaves the hand. 8 − 2 = 6 The sheet stays open.
  4. Tap Hedge Healer's 5 Sway. They join the party (command 2 of 2). 6 − 5 = 1
  5. Trading set Move to 0 and used the turn's action. Tap End turn.

4. Recover from wounds

The dev=night scenario: Night 1, a Wound in hand with seven other cards. Shots 19 and 13.

  1. At the start of the turn, before moving or playing anything, the action bar shows Recover (shot 19). End turn is dim.
  2. Tap Recover. The sheet offers a Give up button under each non-wound card (shot 13).
  3. Tap Give up under the card you value least. It goes to the discard pile, and every wound in hand goes there too.
  4. Recovering ends your actions: Move is 0 and you cannot fight or trade. Tap End turn to draw back up.

The wounds are not gone: they come back when the discard is reshuffled. Only Heal removes a wound for good.

How a tap flows

data-act→ACTS[...]→dispatch()→E.act()→save()→render()

Pure UI state (open sheet, battle targets, map view, hero pick) lives in ui, not in the saved game. Sheet-only changes call renderSheet() without a full render. To add a control: give it data-act="name" plus data-* arguments, add ACTS.name, and let the engine validate.

Undo

dispatch pushes a JSON snapshot before every accepted action. When state.irrev changes (a draw, a reveal, a shuffle, starting a battle), the history is cleared, so hidden information can never be undone. undo() pops one snapshot or toasts “Nothing to undo (new information was revealed)”.

Known limits

  • Checked in headless Chrome only, at 660 and 760 px tall.
  • One real-phone fault found and fixed so far: after a hex tap opened a sheet on pointerup, the browser's follow-up click landed on the new backdrop and closed the sheet. swallowClickUntil now drops trusted clicks for 450 ms after a hex tap.
  • Real touch behaviour, safe-area insets and the installed (home-screen) look are otherwise unverified.
  • Abandon this game has no confirmation step.
